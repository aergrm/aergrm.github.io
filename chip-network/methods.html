Methods, evidence dates, and limitations

The project deliberately separates evidence that measures different relationships. A newer publication date does not automatically imply newer bilateral trade values, and trade flows do not directly reveal firm ownership, design IP, or strategic partnerships.

1. Firm-network evidence through 2025

The default layer summarizes Köse et al. (2026), which reconstructs a temporal, multi-relational semiconductor network from company webpages archived in Common Crawl. The study distinguishes supply/buy, partnership, and ownership relationships and reports a network of more than 1,300 linked firms. The map uses only findings reported in the paper: regional centrality trajectories, selected firm alignment, the rise of NVIDIA and TSMC, and the contraction of newly observed U.S.–China links in 2024–2025.

The project does not reproduce or reverse-engineer the paper’s firm-level edge list. The authors state that aggregated derived data and code are available upon request. Consequently, this layer uses qualitative trend categories rather than invented numerical precision.

Source: Köse et al. (2026).

2. Strategic cooperation through July 2026

Records cover official bilateral agreements and selected multilateral frameworks. Each record includes its date, participating states, cooperation type, status, and a source link. Cooperation is not treated as equivalent to trade: an agreement may diversify dependence, deepen integration with a dominant ecosystem, or remain unimplemented.

3. 2024 trade baseline

The trade layer uses CEPII BACI HS22 version 202601. It aggregates HS 8542 integrated circuits, HS 8486 semiconductor manufacturing equipment, HS 381800 doped electronic-grade materials, and non-photovoltaic HS 8541 semiconductor devices. Photovoltaic cells and modules are excluded to prevent solar trade from being misclassified as chip dependence.

Measures include bilateral import and export values, partner concentration, top-partner shares, and great-power exposure. BACI is retained as the latest globally harmonized and reconciled country-to-country baseline, but it is never labeled as current 2026 data.

Source: CEPII BACI.

4. Theoretical organization

The project follows the cross-network approach developed by Beaumier and Cartwright: semiconductor power is distributed across design, materials, manufacturing equipment, and assembled chips. Centrality in one network may create leverage in another. The website therefore avoids collapsing all forms of dependence into one score.

Related sources: Beaumier and Cartwright (2024); Li et al. (2025); Malkin and He (2024).

5. Main limitations

  • The 2025 firm network is observable and web-disclosed, not a complete census. It is unweighted and may underrepresent smaller firms.
  • The 2024 customs layer cannot observe design licensing, software, services, ownership, contract manufacturing, or export-control authorization.
  • Official cooperation records identify commitments, not necessarily implementation or realized production capacity.
  • “Middle power” is an analytical category used for this project, not an official classification.
