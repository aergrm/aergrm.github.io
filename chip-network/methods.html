Methods, sources, and limitations

The project separates cooperation, implementation evidence, customs trade, curated capability profiles, and published firm-network evidence because they measure different relationships and have different observation dates.

1. Research scope and country sample

The page asks how selected middle powers use specialized capabilities and international cooperation to manage dependence in the semiconductor value chain. Countries are included because they possess a significant semiconductor capability, are pursuing an identifiable semiconductor industrial strategy, or participate in documented international semiconductor cooperation.

The interface distinguishes three project categories: selected middle powers, major semiconductor hubs, and external anchors. These are analytical groupings rather than official classifications. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and the Netherlands are displayed as major hubs because their positions in fabrication, equipment, materials, or design are systemically important and are not well represented by a single middle-power label.

2. Semiconductor cooperation through July 2026

The cooperation dataset contains official bilateral agreements and selected multilateral frameworks from 2021 through July 2026. A record is included when an official source identifies semiconductors as a direct object of cooperation or as an explicit component of a broader technology, industrial, or supply-chain framework.

Each record includes the agreement date, participants, type, scope, value-chain stages, status, implementation evidence, funding information when stated, an official source, and the date last verified. The scope field distinguishes semiconductor-specific agreements from broader frameworks in which semiconductors are one explicit component.

Cooperation types are supply-chain resilience, research and technology, ecosystem and workforce, investment and manufacturing, and trusted-network coordination. Status categories are announced, signed, funded, open call, under implementation, and operational. Status is coded conservatively from the official source; a signed statement is not treated as operational without evidence of continuing institutions, funded activity, implementation, or recurring operation.

Map lines represent documented relationships, not their effectiveness, economic value, or political importance. Multilateral agreements may generate several map lines for visualization, while the underlying dataset retains a single agreement record.

The compiled cooperation data and detailed coding materials are not publicly downloadable while the related article is under review.

3. Signature-versus-implementation chart

The implementation chart groups the visible records into four display categories: announced or signed, funded or open call, under implementation, and operational. Counts update with the selected country, year, cooperation type, scope, status, and value-chain-stage filters.

The chart is descriptive. “Operational” means the official source documents recurring institutions, continuing activity, or an operating programme. It does not establish effectiveness, production output, commercial success, or causal impact.

4. Country capabilities, dependencies, and representative entities

Country profiles summarize value-chain roles, capabilities, and dependencies. They are descriptive rather than prescriptive and do not recommend national strategies. Each profile links to official national, ministry, investment-agency, firm, or public research sources used to document the country’s semiconductor position.

Release 4.3 provides curated profiles for India, Malaysia, and Canada. Entity entries distinguish headquarters, the location of the facility or activity, ownership or institutional type, value-chain role, and operational status. A foreign-owned facility located in a country is therefore treated as territorial production capacity rather than automatic national control over technology, intellectual property, investment decisions, or suppliers.

The entity lists are representative and non-exhaustive. Their purpose is to illustrate the profile logic, not count every firm or rank national semiconductor power. Filters classify entities by stage, ownership form, and visible implementation status using the public descriptions displayed on the page.

5. Capability-gap strip

The capability-gap strip summarizes seven stages: design and intellectual property, materials, equipment, fabrication, packaging and testing, research and skills, and markets. Each stage is assigned one qualitative label: established domestic capability, partial or specialized capability, or predominantly external dependence. A diamond marks stages targeted by visible international cooperation.

The labels are interpretive syntheses of the visible country profiles, trade patterns, and representative entities. They are not quantitative scores, measures of self-sufficiency, or claims that all activities within a stage share the same level of capability.

6. Bilateral semiconductor trade in 2024

The trade layer uses CEPII BACI HS22 version 202601. BACI reconciles exporter and importer reports from UN Comtrade and provides globally comparable bilateral product-level flows. Values are converted from thousands of current U.S. dollars to current U.S. dollars.

The product basket contains HS 8542 integrated circuits, HS 8486 semiconductor manufacturing equipment, HS 381800 doped electronic-grade materials, and HS 8541 semiconductor devices excluding photovoltaic cells and modules classified under 854142 and 854143. The exclusions prevent solar-panel trade from being interpreted as semiconductor-network dependence.

The page reports imports, exports, balance, partner concentration using the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index, top-partner shares, effective partner counts, partner rankings, and product composition. The Taiwan node uses BACI/UN code 490, “Asia not elsewhere specified,” as a documented proxy. The European Union node aggregates EU-27 members while excluding the separately mapped Netherlands from the map node to avoid duplicate flows.

Product filters recalculate displayed link values and country product totals. Partner rankings and HHI remain based on the full four-product basket and are labeled accordingly. The trade layer does not observe design licensing, software, services, ownership, investment, export-control authorizations, domestic absorption, or production technology.

Source: CEPII BACI.

7. Published firm-network findings through 2025

The research section summarizes Köse et al. (2026), which reconstructs temporal supply/buy, partnership, and ownership relationships from company webpages archived through 2025. The study reports a network of more than 1,300 linked firms, model-validation results, regional collaboration trajectories, and selected firm and regional centrality measures.

The page identifies the period, measure, finding, and figure or table location in the source paper. It uses Figure 1 and Table 1 for network construction and validation, Figure 3 for regional collaboration trajectories, Figure 4 for centrality results, and Supplementary Figure S3.2 for the country composition of newly observed links.

The complete derived firm-level edge list is not publicly downloadable. The site therefore does not reconstruct, estimate, or simulate a country-to-country firm network. Source: Köse et al. (2026).

8. Interactive calculations and shareable views

  • Trade threshold: removes displayed links below the selected U.S.-dollar value.
  • Partner share: when a country is selected, a link is divided by the selected country’s imports, exports, or combined trade in the active product groups. Without a selected country, the share is calculated against the exporter’s active-product exports.
  • Import HHI: the sum of squared import-partner shares in the full four-product basket.
  • Effective partners: the reciprocal of HHI.
  • Country comparison: reports cooperation counts through the selected year and 2024 trade measures; it does not create a composite score.
  • Copy this view: copies the current URL, including supported country, comparison, evidence-layer, year, filter, and case-profile parameters.

9. Main limitations

  • The cooperation dataset is a documented release, not a claim that every relevant agreement worldwide has been captured.
  • Official agreements identify commitments and institutions, not necessarily realized investment, implementation quality, production output, or effectiveness.
  • Countries with more transparent institutions or more active public communication may produce more visible records.
  • Country-profile capabilities and dependencies are qualitative syntheses of public sources.
  • The representative entity lists are not firm censuses and should not be used to compare countries by counts.
  • Customs trade is lagged and cannot measure intellectual property, services, ownership, technical sophistication, or supply-chain substitutability.
  • The web-reconstructed firm network may underrepresent smaller firms and relationships not disclosed on company webpages.
  • Map position, line width, status counts, and capability labels should not be read as causal evidence or as an overall measure of national semiconductor power.

10. Availability and citation

The public page presents an interactive research preview. The compiled dataset, detailed coding materials, research memo, and replication files are being withheld while the related manuscript is under journal review. Public source documents remain linked where they support visible records, country profiles, or published findings.
