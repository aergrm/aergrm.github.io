Methods, sources, and limitations

The project separates cooperation, customs trade, curated capability profiles, and published firm-network evidence because they measure different relationships and have different observation dates.

1. Research scope and country sample

The page asks how selected middle powers use specialized capabilities and international cooperation to manage dependence in the semiconductor value chain. Countries are included because they possess a significant semiconductor capability, are pursuing an identifiable semiconductor industrial strategy, or participate in documented international semiconductor cooperation.

The interface distinguishes three analytical groups: selected middle powers, major semiconductor hubs, and external anchors. These are project categories rather than official classifications. Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, and the Netherlands are displayed as major hubs because their positions in fabrication, equipment, materials, or design are systemically important and not well represented by a single middle-power label.

2. Semiconductor cooperation through July 2026

The cooperation dataset contains official bilateral agreements and selected multilateral frameworks from 2021 through July 2026. A record is included when an official source identifies semiconductors as a direct object of cooperation or as an explicit component of a broader technology, industrial, or supply-chain framework.

Each record includes the agreement date, participants, type, scope, value-chain stages, status, implementation evidence, funding information when stated, an official source, and the date last verified. The scope field distinguishes semiconductor-specific agreements from broader frameworks in which semiconductors are one explicit component.

Cooperation types are supply-chain resilience, research and technology, ecosystem and workforce, investment and manufacturing, and trusted-network coordination. Status categories are signed, funded, open call, under implementation, operational, and announced. Status is coded conservatively from the official source; a signed statement is not treated as operational without evidence of continuing institutions, funded activity, or implementation.

Map lines represent documented relationships, not their effectiveness, economic value, or political importance. Multilateral agreements may generate several map lines for visualization, while the underlying dataset retains a single agreement record.

The compiled cooperation data and coding materials are not publicly downloadable while the related article is under review.

3. Country capabilities, representative firms, and institutions

Release 4.2 adds curated profiles for India, Malaysia, and Canada. These profiles connect three questions: what capabilities are present in the country, what external inputs or relationships remain necessary, and what international cooperation is intended to provide.

The firm and institution lists are representative and non-exhaustive. They are not a complete firm census and are not used to calculate national scores. An entity is included when an official government, investment-agency, public-institution, or company source documents a semiconductor-related facility, research function, design activity, fabrication role, packaging or testing capability, or commercialization service relevant to the case.

Each visible record separates the entity’s headquarters country from the location of the facility or activity. It also distinguishes domestic firms, foreign-owned operating facilities, joint ventures, public research institutions, and shared-access infrastructure. Status labels distinguish operational activity from pilot operation, announced expansion, and implementation still in progress.

Ownership and territorial production are not treated as equivalent. A foreign-owned plant can expand production and employment inside a country without transferring control over intellectual property, equipment, strategic investment decisions, or customers. Likewise, a research centre or pilot line documents access and learning infrastructure, not high-volume commercial capacity.

The case profiles were last checked on 1 August 2026. The underlying curated records are displayed on the page but are not offered as a downloadable firm-level dataset.

4. Bilateral semiconductor trade in 2024

The trade layer uses CEPII BACI HS22 version 202601. BACI reconciles exporter and importer reports from UN Comtrade and provides globally comparable bilateral product-level flows. Values are converted from thousands of current U.S. dollars to current U.S. dollars.

The product basket contains HS 8542 integrated circuits, HS 8486 semiconductor manufacturing equipment, HS 381800 doped electronic-grade materials, and HS 8541 semiconductor devices excluding photovoltaic cells and modules classified under 854142 and 854143. The exclusions prevent solar-panel trade from being interpreted as semiconductor-network dependence.

The page reports imports, exports, balance, partner concentration using the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index, top-partner shares, effective partner counts, partner rankings, and separate import and export product composition. The Taiwan node uses BACI/UN code 490, “Asia not elsewhere specified,” as a documented proxy. The European Union node aggregates EU-27 members while excluding the separately mapped Netherlands from the map node to avoid duplicate flows.

Product filters recalculate displayed link values and country product totals. Partner rankings and HHI remain based on the full four-product basket and are labeled accordingly. The trade layer does not observe design licensing, software, services, ownership, investment, export-control authorizations, or production technology.

Source: CEPII BACI.

5. Published firm-network findings through 2025

The research section summarizes Köse et al. (2026), which reconstructs temporal supply/buy, partnership, and ownership relationships from company webpages archived through 2025. The study reports a network of more than 1,300 linked firms, model-validation results, regional collaboration trajectories, and selected firm and regional centrality measures.

The page identifies the period, measure, finding, and figure or table location in the source paper. It uses Figure 1 and Table 1 for network construction and validation, Figure 3 for regional collaboration trajectories, Figure 4 for centrality results, and Supplementary Figure S3.2 for the country composition of newly observed links.

The complete derived firm-level edge list is not publicly downloadable. The site therefore does not reconstruct, estimate, or simulate a country-to-country firm network. Source: Köse et al. (2026).

6. Interactive calculations

  • Trade threshold: removes displayed links below the selected U.S.-dollar value.
  • Partner share: when a country is selected, a link is divided by the selected country’s imports, exports, or combined trade in the active product groups. Without a selected country, the share is calculated against the exporter’s active-product exports.
  • Import HHI: the sum of squared import-partner shares in the full four-product basket.
  • Effective partners: the reciprocal of HHI.
  • Country comparison: reports cooperation counts through the selected year and 2024 trade measures; it does not create a composite score.
  • Capability–trade–cooperation matrix: places a qualitative capability profile beside the 2024 trade baseline and the documented cooperation response. It illustrates different starting positions rather than estimating causal effects.

7. Main limitations

  • The cooperation dataset is a documented release, not a claim that every relevant agreement worldwide has been captured.
  • Official agreements identify commitments and institutions, not necessarily realized investment, implementation quality, or production output.
  • The representative entity lists do not capture every firm, facility, university, supplier relationship, or private investment.
  • Company and government statements may emphasize planned capacity; the page therefore separates operational activity from projects under implementation.
  • Country-profile capabilities and dependencies are qualitative syntheses of cited sources.
  • Customs trade is lagged and cannot measure intellectual property, services, ownership, technical sophistication, or supply-chain substitutability.
  • The web-reconstructed firm network may underrepresent smaller firms and relationships not disclosed on company webpages.
  • Map position, firm count, and line width should not be read as causal evidence or an overall measure of national semiconductor power.

8. Availability

The public page presents an interactive research preview. The compiled cooperation dataset, detailed coding materials, research memo, and replication files are being withheld while the related manuscript is under journal review. Public source documents remain linked where they support visible cooperation records, country profiles, representative entity records, or published findings.
