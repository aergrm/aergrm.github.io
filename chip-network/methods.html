Methods and data documentation

Middle Powers in the Global Semiconductor Network

Release: research prototype, July 2026. The project is designed as expandable public research infrastructure rather than a final measurement product.

1. Research objective

The map asks how states outside the two principal technology superpowers use specialized capabilities, international cooperation, market access, capital, and political alignment to manage semiconductor dependence. It combines position in the value chain, formal cooperation, and structural exposure.

2. Country selection

The initial sample contains technologically consequential middle powers and emerging entrants. “Middle power” is an analytical category used for this project, not an official legal classification. The United States, China, and the European Union are displayed as external anchors because middle-power strategies cannot be interpreted without them.

4. Cooperation records

Bilateral records are included when an official government source identifies a semiconductor partnership, alliance, memorandum, or formal cooperation framework. Multilateral entries are coded as membership records rather than converted into an artificial set of bilateral agreements. Every record in the public data file contains a source URL.

5. Structural corridors

Corridors indicate an important direction of dependence or complementarity. They are qualitative codings based on value-chain specialization, publicly documented production relationships, and the research literature. They are not bilateral dollar flows. The intensity score ranges from one to five and is intended for visualization, not statistical inference.

6. Live trade data

Country profiles can query the UN Comtrade preview API for annual exports and imports under HS heading 8542, integrated circuits. The browser requests the reporter’s trade with the world for the selected year. The API can be rate-limited, and countries differ in reporting schedules and classifications.

HS 8542 cannot measure the complete semiconductor value chain. Equipment, materials, wafers, intellectual property, design services, foreign ownership, and contract manufacturing require additional product codes and non-customs data. Future releases should process BACI or Comtrade data offline and publish reproducible bilateral matrices for HS 8486, 8541, 8542, 3818, and carefully selected six-digit inputs.

7. Exposure scores

The four exposure indicators are ordinal research codings from zero to five. They summarize strategic reliance on the United States, China, Europe, and Asian production networks. They should be treated as hypotheses to be replaced by transparent quantitative measures, including partner concentration, import reliance, market dependence, and technology-control exposure.

8. Principal sources

  • UN Comtrade API documentation
  • OECD, Mapping the Semiconductor Value Chain (2025)
  • CEPII BACI database
  • Official agreement sources linked individually in the public data file and country profiles.

9. Reproducibility and expansion

The website is a static GitHub Pages application. All substantive records are visible in data.js. A full research release should add a versioned CSV dataset, an R or Python pipeline for trade processing, source-archiving fields, inter-coder rules for agreement classification, and downloadable country-level indicators.
