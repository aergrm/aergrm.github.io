# Chip-network data notes

## Canonical cooperation data

`cooperation-data.js`, together with the release-specific additions in `cooperation-data-extensions.js`, `cooperation-data-release-4-3-2.js`, `cooperation-data-release-4-3-3.js`, `cooperation-data-release-4-3-4.js`, `cooperation-data-release-4-3-5.js`, and `cooperation-data-release-4-3-6.js`, is the data source used by the current interactive application.

The older `COOPERATION` array in `data.js` is **deprecated**. It is retained only for backward compatibility with earlier prototypes and must not be updated, cited, or used for current agreement counts. New cooperation records must follow the schema used by `COOPERATION_DATA`: date, participants, map pairs, type, scope, value-chain stages, status, implementation evidence, funding, official source URL, and verification date.

Release 4.3.1 expanded the displayed cooperation data from 21 to 31 records, corrected the start of the Japan–U.S. JUCIP semiconductor record to May 4, 2022, and added official records involving the Quad, Taiwan, South Korea, Singapore, the United Kingdom, Vietnam, Canada, and India.

Release 4.3.2 expanded the displayed cooperation data from 31 to 39 records. It added official records for the U.S.–Singapore Partnership for Growth and Innovation, Japan–Canada industrial science and technology cooperation, Vietnam–Japan semiconductor research, the U.S.–UAE AI Acceleration Partnership, Malaysia–Netherlands semiconductor cooperation, Korea–UAE AI and semiconductor cooperation, the U.S.–Saudi Strategic AI Partnership, and India–Vietnam digital technology cooperation.

Release 4.3.3 expanded the displayed cooperation data from 39 to 47 records. It added the EU–Japan Memorandum of Cooperation on Semiconductors, the UK–Taiwan Enhanced Trade Partnership semiconductor track, India–France Horizon 2047 strategic-technology cooperation, the U.S.–India TRUST initiative, the France–UAE AI Framework Agreement, the Singapore–Vietnam Comprehensive Strategic Partnership, the announced India–Malaysia structured semiconductor cooperation process, and the industry-led India–Singapore semiconductor corridor.

Release 4.3.4 expanded the displayed cooperation data from 47 to 55 records. It added the Quad Semiconductor Supply Chains Contingency Network, the G7 Semiconductors Point of Contact Group, the Canada–Germany Joint Declaration of Intent on Semiconductor Cooperation, the Australia–Canada–India Technology and Innovation Partnership, the Canada–Taiwan joint semiconductor and AI research program, the UK–Taiwan Semiconductor Joint Skills Project, Japan–Singapore Strategic Partnership semiconductor cooperation, and the Taiwan–Japan NSTC–RIKEN semiconductor research program.

Release 4.3.4 also updated implementation evidence for EU–Japan semiconductor cooperation, EU–India semiconductor cooperation, and EU–Republic of Korea joint chip research. It added the European Union to the Pax Silica record following the Commission's June 25, 2026 signature. Germany is represented by the EU anchor on the map because the current visualization does not contain a separate German node. The G7 record similarly uses the EU anchor to represent continental European members that do not have separate map nodes.

Release 4.3.5 expanded the displayed cooperation data from 55 to 67 records. It added the India–Germany Semiconductor Ecosystem Partnership; the U.S.–Taiwan Agreement on Trade and Investment semiconductor partnership; the UK–Netherlands Innovation Partnership semiconductor track; the Japan–Italy Memorandum of Cooperation on Supply Chain Resilience; the Australia–Vietnam Strategic Technologies Centre; EU–Vietnam Comprehensive Strategic Partnership semiconductor cooperation; the European Semicon Coalition; the Canada–UK Scientific Research and Innovation Memorandum of Understanding; Japan–India Digital Partnership 2.0; the UK–Republic of Korea Digital Partnership semiconductor track; the India–U.S. AI Opportunity Partnership; and the India–Israel Critical and Emerging Technologies initiative.

Release 4.3.5 also updated the Pax Silica record to reflect additional 2026 participation by the United Arab Emirates, India, the European Union, and the Netherlands, and updated the 2023 UK–Japan Semiconductor Partnership with June 2026 evidence linking the UK Semiconductor Centre to Rapidus and the wider Frontier Technology Partnership. Italy and Germany are represented through the EU anchor where no separate country node exists. The Semicon Coalition is represented through the Netherlands and EU nodes because the current map does not display all EU member states separately.

Release 4.3.6 expands the displayed cooperation data from 67 to 74 records. It adds the NSF–NSTC Advanced Chip Engineering Design and Fabrication program; Australia–Japan semiconductor supply-chain cooperation; the Taiwan–Germany Scientific and Technological Cooperation Arrangement; the Taiwan–France Science and Technology Cooperation Convention; the Taiwan–Canada Science, Technology and Innovation Arrangement; the Taiwan–Australia Science and Technology Arrangement; and the Taiwan–Israel semiconductor design and next-generation materials research program.

Release 4.3.6 strengthens coverage of Taiwan-centered cooperation that is documented through science and technology agencies rather than trade ministries. Germany and France are represented through the EU anchor because the current visualization does not contain separate German or French nodes. The Taiwan–Israel record uses the date of the official public call page because the source does not provide a separate signing date for that bilateral research cycle.

All extension files are idempotent: they add only records whose identifiers are not already present, sort the combined dataset, and ask the interface to rerender. Each public expansion is retained in a separate release layer so its provenance remains auditable.
