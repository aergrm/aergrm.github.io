# Measurement notes from the 2024–2026 semiconductor literature

Release 4.1 uses the uploaded literature to revise measurement rules, not to create unsupported country scores.

1. **Separate value-chain stages.** Materials, equipment, final chips, design, and services are different functions with different leaders and dependencies. Aggregate semiconductor trade hides specialization.
2. **Do not equate customs trade with capability.** Trade omits design services, domestic absorption, ownership, licensing, production capacity, and technological differences within product codes.
3. **Treat dependence as multilateral and stage-specific.** Direct import shares miss indirect exposure through upstream equipment and materials. Dependence may be two-sided because suppliers also rely on markets, technology, and inputs controlled by importers.
4. **Keep cooperation distinct from outcomes.** Agreements may create channels for FDI, research, licensing, workforce exchange, or technology transfer, but implementation and knowledge transfer are deliberate and not automatic.
5. **Separate state and firm capacity.** Territorial industrial policy depends on firms that possess strategic know-how. The location of a firm or facility does not imply that the state controls all underlying technology.
6. **Use weighted and meso-level network measures.** In-strength, out-strength, HHI, effective partners, betweenness, HITS, and community detection answer different questions.
7. **Model heterogeneous resilience.** Partner diversity, substitution, inventories, and tolerance vary across countries; uniform shock assumptions are not appropriate for substantive risk simulations.
8. **Recognize intermediary transmission.** Shocks can travel through direct trade, regional clustering, intermediary nodes, and across product layers.
9. **Avoid causal language.** Trade-network associations and cooperation records do not identify the causal effect of political alignment or agreements without a separate research design.
10. **Do not treat localization as completed sovereignty.** Subsidized territorial capacity may remain dependent on foreign firms, skills, equipment, intellectual property, and continuing public support.
